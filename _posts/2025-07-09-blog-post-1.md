I recently attended an AI in Finance conference and was surprised to discover that many researchers are using chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting incorrectly. This powerful technique can significantly improve reasoning in LLMs — but only when implemented properly.

<div class="key-takeaways">
<h3>Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li>Zero-shot CoT requires <strong>two separate prompting rounds</strong>, not one.</li>
<li>Combining reasoning and the final answer in a single prompt introduces <strong>answer bleeding</strong>.</li>
<li>CoT is for structured reasoning; <strong>explainable prompting</strong> is for human-readable justification — know when to use which.</li>
</ul>
</div>

## What is Zero-Shot Chain-of-Thought?

Zero-shot CoT involves **two distinct rounds of prompting** without using any task-specific examples:

1. **Round 1** — Prompt the model to generate step-by-step reasoning.
2. **Round 2** — Explicitly ask for the final answer based on that reasoning.

This differs from few-shot CoT, which includes labeled examples.

<div class="callout callout-info">
<strong>Why two rounds?</strong>
Separating reasoning from the final answer prevents the model from "anchoring" on a premature conclusion and then rationalizing backwards.
</div>

## Example Question

Consider the question:
*"A company just announced a 20% dividend increase while simultaneously reporting declining revenues. Is this news good or bad?"*

### Incorrect: Single-Stage Approach

<div class="callout callout-warning">
<strong>Common Mistake</strong>
Many researchers combine reasoning and answer extraction into a single prompt. This allows the model to peek at its own conclusion while still "reasoning."
</div>

<span class="code-label code-label-wrong">WRONG</span>

```python
# Single prompt — reasoning and answer are entangled
response = llm.generate(
    prompt="Let's think step by step: A company just announced..."
)
# Output includes both reasoning AND final answer in one response
```

### Correct: Two-Stage Approach

<span class="code-label code-label-correct">CORRECT</span>

```python
# STEP 1: Trigger reasoning only
reasoning_prompt = (
    "Q: A company announced a 20% dividend increase "
    "but declining revenues... "
    "A: Let's think step by step."
)
intermediate_response = llm.generate(reasoning_prompt)

# STEP 2: Extract final answer from the reasoning
answer_prompt = f"""
Based on this analysis: '{intermediate_response}'
Is the news good or bad? Answer ONLY 'good' or 'bad'."""
final_answer = llm.generate(answer_prompt)
```

## Why This Matters

| Problem | Single-Stage | Two-Stage (Correct) |
|---|---|---|
| Answer bleeding | Model sees its conclusion while reasoning | Reasoning is isolated from the answer |
| Transparency | Tangled output, hard to audit | Clean separation of logic and decision |
| Hallucination risk | Higher — model may fabricate justifications | Lower — reasoning is evaluated independently |


## Explainable Prompting vs. Chain-of-Thought

Although both aim to improve interpretability, they serve different purposes:

<table class="comparison-table">
<thead>
<tr><th>Feature</th><th>Explainable Prompting</th><th>Chain-of-Thought</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>Goal</strong></td><td>Human-readable justification</td><td>Structured multi-step reasoning</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Output</strong></td><td>Single response with embedded rationale</td><td>Two-step: reasoning → answer</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Best for</strong></td><td>Summaries, end-user reports</td><td>Complex logic, quantitative analysis</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Prompt style</strong></td><td>"Explain why…"</td><td>"Let's think step by step"</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<div class="callout callout-tip">
<strong>Rule of thumb</strong>
Use <em>explainable prompting</em> when the audience needs to understand the conclusion. Use <em>CoT</em> when correctness and traceability matter more than readability.
</div>


## Implementation Checklist

1. **Never** include "so the answer is..." in the initial reasoning prompt.
2. **Always** split into two separate prompts/responses.
3. **Validate** intermediate reasoning before extracting the final answer.
